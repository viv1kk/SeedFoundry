# Vendor contract notes

Notes from the procurement team on how the main vendors' contracts treat returned seats. Background for timing; the method itself is in the four core files.

- **Renewal windows.** Most subscriptions renew once a year. Seats can be reduced only at renewal, usually with 60 to 90 days' notice. A seat found after the notice date is saved a year later.
- **True-ups.** Enterprise agreements count seats once a year at the true-up date and bill any growth for the whole year. Reclaiming before the true-up avoids paying for growth that is not real.
- **Minimum commitments.** Some agreements set a floor on the seat count. Seats below the floor cannot be returned, only given to someone who needs one.
- **Shelfware.** Seats bought in bulk for a project that has ended are the largest single source of unassigned seats.
- **Unpriced products.** Products bought through a reseller, or bundled into another agreement, often have no price on the contract. Procurement can supply prices on request, product by product.
- **Leavers.** Seats are not released when someone leaves. The manager has to raise a request, and it is often missed.
